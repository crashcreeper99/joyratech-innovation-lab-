# Public Documentation and Intellectual Property

This page is a practical documentation rule, not legal advice.

## Appropriate for the public repository

- the problem and intended user;
- high-level goals and project status;
- prior art and public research;
- non-confidential learning exercises;
- test methods and results that are safe to disclose;
- demonstrations already approved for publication;
- honest limitations and next steps.

## Keep private until reviewed

- potentially novel circuit or system architectures;
- detailed schematics, board files, algorithms, source code, or manufacturing methods that may support a patent filing;
- credentials, private keys, personal data, and participant records;
- confidential work from collaborators, employers, classes, or competitions;
- unreviewed security-sensitive designs.

## Before publishing a major technical update

1. Identify exactly what is new.
2. Search for existing products and prior art.
3. Decide whether publication could affect patent options.
4. Confirm that every collaborator agrees to the disclosure.
5. Remove secrets and personal data.
6. Publish only the material intentionally approved.

A public repository without a license does not automatically grant others broad permission to reuse the work. A license can be selected later when the project's sharing goals are clear.
