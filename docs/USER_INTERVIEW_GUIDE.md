# User Interview Guide

The purpose of an interview is to understand a person's real experience before proposing a solution.

## Before the interview

- Ask permission to take notes or record.
- Explain that this is early research, not a finished product.
- Do not promise a medical, accessibility, or universal-compatibility outcome.
- Avoid collecting sensitive information that is not necessary.

## Questions

1. Tell me about the last time this problem happened.
2. What were you trying to accomplish?
3. What made it difficult?
4. What did you do instead?
5. What tools or assistance do you use now?
6. What works well about the current approach?
7. What is frustrating, slow, expensive, or inaccessible?
8. How often does this happen?
9. What would a meaningful improvement look like?
10. Who else should I learn from?

## Avoid leading questions

Instead of “Would you use a thought-to-text headband?” ask “How do you communicate when typing is difficult or impossible?”

Instead of “Would universal headphones solve this?” ask “How do you move audio or controls between your devices today?”

## After the interview

Record:

- observed problem;
- current workaround;
- frequency and severity;
- exact evidence versus your interpretation;
- new assumptions to test;
- whether another conversation is justified.

Do not publish a participant's identity or sensitive details without clear permission.
